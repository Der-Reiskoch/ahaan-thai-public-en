The story behind Ahaan Thai

From one cookbook to a whole encyclopedia

Great to have you here! I’m Jens, and since 2011 I have been cooking and writing on Der Reiskoch, mostly about Thai food. If you want to know more about me, you’ll find it there. Here I’ll tell you how Ahaan Thai came about.

How it all started

Many years ago I got the German edition of Thailand: The Cookbook by Jean-Pierre Gabriel for my birthday. It is a big, beautiful book full of Thai recipes, from classics like green curry to unusual dishes like omelette with ant eggs. What bothered me from the start, though, was that every dish is listed with nothing but a German description and its name in Thai script. How it is pronounced, and what you need to ask for in Thailand to get it on your plate, the book never tells you.

So I started looking the names up, writing them down in a transcription you can actually read, and cooking the dishes. Those notes became Ahaan Thai.

What you’ll find here

For every dish you’ll find its name in Thai script and in transcription, the other spellings you might come across, and a short description. Almost always there are recipes too, for example from cookbooks, blogs, cooking videos or the Lanna food directory of Chiang Mai University. I introduce each of these sources on a page of its own.

If you’d rather browse by origin, the regions will help. And if a word keeps turning up in the names of dishes, just look it up in the dictionary.

An encyclopedia like this is never finished. Is a dish missing, do you know a good recipe or do you have a photo I may show? The page The Encyclopedia Needs You! explains how you can take part.
